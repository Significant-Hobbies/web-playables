# Ember Road

Ember Road lets a player lead Rook, a companion and a pet through five chapters by shaping a build, fighting groups, choosing oaths and returning stronger after defeat.

The owner selected the cartoon idle-RPG reference on 16 November 2026 after rejecting the sidebar-and-cards composition as a SaaS dashboard. The reference establishes chunky characters, thick outlines, saturated colors, illustrated chapter dioramas, large icon navigation and a dominant Start action. Its artwork, economy, offers and progression are not copied.

The chapter hub is a game screen. The current chapter and its company own the center. Top chrome shows comparative company Power, gold and account access; Damage remains a separate combat attribute. Power combines offense, defense, company and the equipped skill, includes journey oaths, and does not change with current health or cooldowns. Side actions open real story, daily and weekly quests or company inspection. The bottom dock opens equipment, companions, skills and pets. Detail surfaces are modal; the document never scrolls. Desktop1440×900 and1280×720 are primary. Smaller widths retain usable start/navigation and internally scrolling dialogs.

Fleet React components continue to own buttons, dialogs, progress, badges and switches. Game-specific tokens, artwork and motion give these controls their identity. Original SVG game icons are art inside Fleet buttons. Strong rarity colors remain paired with names and shapes. Notifications appear only for real available rewards. Equipment stays camp-only; inspections pause combat; retreat remains explicit and preserves permanent progress.

Original cartoon sheets retain ready, anticipation, contact, recovery and walking poses. Combat uses the same model timings, target positions, projectiles, hit reactions and active skills. Camp motion is ambient and stops for platform/user pause, inspections and reduced-motion preference. Each chapter has distinct original environment art. Do not restore the old dashboard structure or mix painterly characters into the cartoon world.

The first visit opens an illustrated three-beat origin story about the dark valley, Mira’s missing father Silas, and Rook’s company. Skip and replay preserve the current journey. Existing journeys return to the paused company hub until the player explicitly resumes; a fresh opening can start chapter one. Small field notes teach automatic combat, active skills, oaths and forging without forcing choices. Seen flags, audio preferences and bounded combat checkpoints share the version-five save. Versions one through four migrate explicitly; older builds protect newer saves as update-required. Migration preserves earned progress and pending attack budgets: a queued v4 enemy attack retains armor-first mitigation once, while subsequent attacks absorb ranged-mitigated damage into the barrier before body armor protects health. Old barriers gain no retroactive Shatterguard charge.

The original Last Light score has synchronized hearth and combat stems. Music starts only after input, stays quiet under dialogue and inspection, and adds its combat layer in battle. Varied event-driven impacts and distinct skill/reward cues share bounded sound buses. Platform/user pause suspends audio and presentation motion; host audio policy always wins. Web controls store music/effect volume and enable states through the same game save. Reduced motion keeps the story readable and removes decorative movement.

New journeys start with Rook alone. Whirlwind awakens through the wind shrine after fight 6 or two chosen oaths. Mira joins through the rescue trail in chapter two after fight 3; Cinder joins through fox tracks in chapter three after fight 9. Earlier signals are story clues granting three Trailmarks. Already-earned discoveries remain permanent, including for existing saves. Discovery is permanent. Players may take a healing spring or elite encampment instead and revisit discoveries on another journey. Campaign chapters retain twelve fights each, with saved, mutually exclusive route decisions at 3, 6 and 9 separate from oath choices. Springs heal, grant Trailmarks and soften the next section; elite trails add armor, health and rear hexers in exchange for double foe Gold and extra Trailmarks; shrines strengthen Rook for the run. A three-fight section leads to the next fork or the region boss.

The company has ten permanent levels. Every level above one adds 2 Rook damage and 5 maximum health to every adventure, while levels 2–5 open the four paid training ranks for discovered companions, pets and skills. Training has five displayed levels including its base level. Gold funds forging, Trailmarks fund company/pet training, and Embers fund skill training. Migrated saves retain earned/equipped system access, coins, perks and active battle checkpoints without a currency grant or retroactive route choice.

After chapter one, daily expeditions rotate through five bundled combat rules on UTC days. Each expedition has six fights and three difficulties. Arrowstorm accelerates rear archers, Ironwatch fields armored sentinels, Glass Cannon doubles damage with fragile foes, Feral Hour rushes the front with fast raiders, and Lantern Run fills the rear with heavy hexers. Chapter two opens the weekly hunt, with three distinct rounds of a rotating boss family: a slow lone warden, a guarded warden, and a faster warden supported by two hexers. Difficulty access follows reached chapters 1, 2 and 4. Event victories do not advance campaign chapters or count as story victories.

The typed adventure registry owns event rules, captured run context and the bounded reward ledger. Each difficulty grants one first-clear Gold/Trailmark/Ember bonus per current period; replays keep foe Gold and completion Gold. The saved UTC high-water marks never move backward. Events keep their original rules across reload and rollover, but an expired run grants only base combat rewards. Starting from a stale poster refreshes the offer instead of silently starting another event. Calendar advancement uses the existing loop and actions, with no new timer or persistence path.

Journey level resets each run; company level, gear and discoveries persist. Chapter departures show the destination, a story letter, goal and twelve-fight structure before explicit embarkation. A brief arrival title uses the existing travel clock. The battlefield and its complete bottom control rail occupy separate layout rows; encounter beads replace plain canvas marker posts. Oath offers use original illustrated emblems inside rarity-colored relic frames with compact binding controls, and have one free, saved refresh. A refresh offers different eligible powers without awarding an oath or changing the current build. Compact power chips stay visible and expose top-layer hover/focus details even over choices. Tips are consumed on first exposure and never restart on a later wave or reload. Dialogs leave the top layer immediately when a choice ends; entry motion never retains an empty closing dialog. Midnight navy, teal and gold replace parchment surfaces while preserving the illustrated cartoon world. Attack anticipation, eased lunges, contact trails and recovery are presentation-only and honor pause and reduced motion.

Build reactions preserve the existing company and oath system. Chapter three reveals Storm Relay and Aegis’s Shatterguard signature; Hunter’s Signal appears after Mira and Cinder are discovered. Registry-owned prerequisites and status feed both the compact belt and current powers. A fresh Aegis carries absorbed charge with its remaining barrier through waves, oaths and forks. Enemy damage breaking it queues up to three shards, capped at 60% absorbed damage and one cast-time Rook attack total. Recasting forfeits charge; terminal outcomes and return to camp clear it. Shards stay within their battle and never heal or trigger another reaction. Piercing third strikes relay a bounded lightning budget; Cinder’s four-second mark adds one snapshotted Mira-arrow bonus. Bracing lets enemies recover after an interrupt. Cyan, violet and orange distinguish these reactions; simultaneous relay hits share one brief critical sound cue.
